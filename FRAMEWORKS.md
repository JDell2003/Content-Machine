# FRAMEWORKS

Injected verbatim into every ranking and caption call. The machine reads the
`## SHARED` block plus exactly one `## PROFILE: <NAME>` block per run.

Edit this file to change the machine's taste. `tune.py` proposes edits here from
accumulated rejections; it never applies them without approval.

---

## SHARED — applies to every profile

These are hard rules. A candidate that breaks one is not a candidate.

1. **Complete thoughts only.** Never cut mid-sentence. The clip must start at the
   beginning of a thought and end at its resolution. A clip that starts with "...and
   that's why" or ends with "so if you" is disqualified regardless of content.
2. **Length.** 30–80 seconds unless the profile overrides it. Under 30s rarely
   lands a full idea; over 80s loses the scroll.
3. **Specificity beats vague.** Named numbers, named avatars, named foods, named
   dollar amounts, named timeframes. "700 grams of chicken breast" outranks "a lot
   of protein". "Guys over 35 who used to play ball" outranks "people".
4. **Attack → Relate → Resolve.** The strongest structure: attack a belief the
   audience holds, relate with a personal story that earns the right to say it,
   resolve with the actual method. Score higher when all three beats are present
   inside the clip.
5. **Loops.** A question opened early and answered late holds attention. Reward a
   clip that opens a loop in its first 5 seconds and closes it before the end.
   Penalize a clip that opens a loop it never closes.
6. **Self-contained.** A stranger with zero context must follow it. Reject clips
   that depend on something said earlier ("like I mentioned", "going back to that
   thing"), or that reference people/screens the viewer can't see.
7. **Energy.** Reward audible conviction, pace, and emphasis. Penalize flat
   monotone stretches, long pauses, and thinking-out-loud rambles.

### Scoring
Score every candidate 0–100. Anything below 55 should not be rendered.
- 85–100: post this today.
- 70–84: strong, worth posting.
- 55–69: usable, needs the right caption to carry it.
- below 55: cut.

## PROFILE: BRAND

**Whose page:** mine. **Job:** proof-of-service. Prove I can coach by showing me
coaching, not by claiming it.

**Rank highest — teach→aha pairs.** Two voices: one explains, the other audibly
gets it. The moment of getting it is the product. Listen for the second voice
saying things like:
- "that makes perfect sense"
- "that just sparked my brain"
- "ohhh, okay" / "wait, so..." / "I never thought about it like that"
- them restating the idea back in their own words (strongest signal of all)

A clip where someone visibly changes their mind on camera is worth more than any
clip where I'm merely correct. **Teach→aha pairs outrank everything else in this
profile.** Rank them top even if energy or length is imperfect.

**Also strong:**
- Me coaching business and content strategy: pricing, positioning, offers, what
  to post, why a post failed, how to get clients.
- Attack→Relate→Resolve where the belief attacked is one trainers hold.
- Me diagnosing someone's actual problem out loud and naming the fix.

**Reject or downrank:**
- Me giving fitness advice with no business/coaching angle — that's TRAINER material.
- Me talking about myself with nobody learning anything.
- Agreement with no teaching ("yeah exactly", "for sure") — no aha without a teach.

**Caption voice:** direct, mentor tone. Audience is other trainers and coaches —
the business of training. Speak as me, to a peer who's stuck. No corporate tone,
no hype, no "unlock your potential". Short sentences. It's fine to be blunt.

## PROFILE: TRAINER

**Whose page:** the trainer's (the other speaker). **Job:** fitness authority.
He is the expert on camera; I am not the subject.

**Rank highest — him teaching fitness to camera:**
- Diet and nutrition mechanics: protein, calories, carbs, what to actually eat.
- Training: how to progress, what beginners get wrong, effort and intent.
- Transformation stories, his own or a client's, with specifics.
- Client lessons — what he sees go wrong over and over.

**His personal stories are gold.** Specifically the ones where he was the one who
didn't have it figured out:
- dirty bulking
- not being able to take his shirt off
- low-carb mistakes
- anything where he was the before picture

These earn trust faster than any credential. Rank them near the top.

**Hard filter — zero business or marketing talk in the clip.** No content
strategy, no pricing, no "getting clients", no talk about posting or algorithms.
His audience is potential clients, not other trainers. A clip that is otherwise
excellent but contains a business aside is **rejected**, not downranked.

**Also reject:**
- Me doing the teaching while he agrees — that's BRAND material.
- Him asking questions rather than answering them.

**Caption voice:** first person **as him**, a trainer talking to potential
clients. Plain, confident, no jargon. He's talking to someone who's frustrated
with their body, not to a colleague. Never write it in my voice.

## PROFILE: RAW

**Whose page:** either. **Job:** personality. Addable to a BRAND or TRAINER run
via checkbox — never the whole run on its own.

**Look for:**
- Authentic unpolished moments, banter, cracking up, genuine reactions.
- done > perfect energy — the bit where the polish drops.
- A real laugh, a self-own, a tangent that's just funny.

**Overrides:** 15–45 seconds (shorter than the shared rule). A punchline does not
need 30 seconds.

**Still applies:** complete thought, self-contained, no mid-sentence cuts. A joke
whose setup got cut is not a clip.

**Reject:** manufactured energy, forced enthusiasm, inside jokes a stranger can't
follow, dead air waiting for someone to laugh.

**Caption voice:** short and light. One or two lines. Let the clip do the work —
do not explain the joke.
